What to Do in the First Hour

The moment the hail stops, most DFW homeowners do one of two things: they either ignore it and hope for the best, or they panic and call their insurance company before they have any idea what they're dealing with. Both are mistakes. Here's what actually helps.

Your first move is simple: write down the date and time the storm hit your neighborhood. This sounds obvious, but insurance claims hinge on storm dates, and if you're filing weeks from now, you want an exact record. Check a local weather app or NOAA Fort Worth's recorded data. The official storm date matters more than your memory of it.

While you're at it, walk around the exterior of your house. Staying on the ground. And take photos and video of anything visible. Dents in your AC unit, dented gutters, damaged window screens, cracked wood fencing. This is not a roof inspection. You are building a timestamped record of the storm's impact on your property from ground level, which costs you nothing and helps the inspection process later.

Don't climb on your roof

Every year DFW homeowners are injured trying to self-inspect after a storm. Hail leaves surfaces slick, and you don't know what structural damage may have occurred. The inspection is free. Leave it to a professional who does this every day. Your job right now is ground-level documentation only.

The Ground Inspection You Can Do Yourself

You don't need to get on the roof to gather useful information. A 10-minute walk around your property. With your phone camera rolling. Tells you a lot and gives your inspector a head start.

The single most reliable ground-level indicator of hail damage is your gutters. After any meaningful hail event, granules will wash off impacted shingles and collect in the gutters and downspout splash blocks. If you see a noticeable buildup of gritty, sand-like material that looks like coarse dark gravel, that's evidence your shingles took a hit. Older roofs shed more granules in general, but post-storm accumulation is different. It's usually heavier and concentrated after rain.

Your AC condenser unit is another reliable tell. The aluminum fins on the outside of the unit dent easily. If you see a pattern of small circular dents across the fin surface, hail impacted your property with enough force to warrant a roof inspection. That same force was hitting your shingles from the same angle, at the same time.

🏠

Granules in Gutters

Sand-like grit collecting in gutters or at downspout exits after a storm is granule loss from shingles. One of the clearest indicators of hail impact. Check every downspout, not just one.

❄️

Dented AC Fins

The aluminum fins on your exterior AC condenser dent easily from hail. A pattern of round dents across the fin surface is a strong sign that hail in the area had enough size and force to warrant a roof inspection. The same storm that hit the AC unit was hitting your shingles at the same time.

🌧️

Dented Gutters & Downspouts

Metal gutters and downspouts show dent patterns clearly. Widespread dimpling. Rather than isolated damage. Indicates a storm event rather than physical impact from another source.

🔍

Window Screens & Trim

Torn or punctured window screens, dented aluminum trim, and cracked vinyl siding fascia are all ground-level indicators that hail size was significant enough to cause roof damage.

If you see two or more of these indicators, there is a strong likelihood your roof sustained damage worth inspecting. If you see none of them, hail may have been too small or too sparse to cause structural damage. But an inspection is still worth scheduling if you heard significant hail on your roof or if your neighbors are reporting damage. For a detailed look at what hail actually does to shingles. And why most of it isn't visible from the ground. See our guide on what hail damage looks like on a roof.

Notify Your Insurer Promptly. And Get Documented Before the Adjuster Arrives

Here's the thing about the insurance claims process that most homeowners don't realize until they're already in it: the first person to document your roof sets the baseline for everything that follows.

When your insurer's adjuster visits your property, they create their own scope based on what they observe. If nobody has done a thorough inspection before they arrive, there's no independent record of what damage existed at the time of the storm. Items that adjusters miss. Granule loss on specific sections, soft spots on shingles, damage to pipe boots and flashing. Can be difficult to add to a claim after the fact. Having your own photo documentation and inspection report gives you an independent record to compare against.

The sequence that works: report your claim promptly to your insurer (check your policy's "Duties After Loss" section for your specific filing deadline. Don't wait), AND schedule a professional inspection right away. Both should happen as soon as possible. The goal isn't to delay filing. It's to have a documented scope in hand before the adjuster arrives.

Texas homeowners often don't realize that their policy has a deadline for reporting damage. The exact window varies by insurer and policy form. You'll find it in your "Duties After Loss" section. But it is not unlimited. Waiting weeks before reporting hail damage can put your claim at risk. When in doubt, report it promptly and let the inspection determine whether there's a compensable claim.

Storm Chasers Will Knock. Here's What to Do

After major hail events in DFW, out-of-town roofing contractors often start showing up in affected neighborhoods quickly. Sometimes within a day or two. They know where the storms hit, and they work door-to-door while the memory of the storm is fresh and homeowners are anxious. This is common storm-chaser behavior, and Collin County homeowners have seen it repeatedly after major hail events.

The problem isn't that they show up. The problem is the pressure tactics: sign today to lock in your spot, we'll waive your deductible, you don't have to pay a thing. That last one is illegal in Texas. State law requires homeowners to pay their property insurance deductible. Contractors cannot waive, absorb, rebate, or cover it. The Texas Department of Insurance specifically warns both homeowners and contractors about this. Anyone who offers to waive your deductible is a reliable signal that the rest of the transaction will be equally problematic.

Be careful with Assignment of Benefits and Direction to Pay forms

If a contractor asks you to sign an "Assignment of Benefits" or "Direction to Pay" before work begins, read it carefully before you do. An AOB can transfer your insurance rights to the contractor, meaning they. Not you. Control how your claim is settled, and this has been associated with inflated claims and contractor-driven disputes. A Direction to Pay may be narrower, but any form that limits your control over your claim should be reviewed carefully. If you are unsure what a document authorizes, ask your insurance agent, an attorney, or a licensed public adjuster before signing. For more on protecting yourself from bad actors, see our guide on storm chasers vs. local roofers in DFW.

The safest approach is simple: don't make any decisions at the door. Get the contractor's business card, tell them you'll call if you're interested, and do your verification first. A legitimate local roofer will not pressure you to sign on the spot. They have a permanent address, an established review history, and they'll be here long after the storm. If a contractor can't give you time to do your homework, that tells you everything you need to know.

What Not to Do

A few things that feel natural but will make this harder:

  • ✗ Don't wait to see if a leak develops. Hail damage rarely causes immediate leaks. It creates micro-fractures that expand through DFW's heat cycles and produce leaks weeks or months later. By then, you may have water intrusion, decking rot, and a more complicated insurance picture. Proactive inspection is always faster and cheaper.
  • ✗ Don't assume your roof is fine because it's new. New construction in Prosper and Celina takes hail just as hard as a 15-year-old roof. Builder-grade shingles are standard, not impact-resistant. If you had significant hail, new construction is worth inspecting.
  • ✗ Don't sign anything from a door-to-door contractor. Take their card, do your verification, and make decisions on your schedule. Not theirs. See our full guide on how to identify and avoid storm chasers in DFW.
  • ✗ Don't skip the inspection because the damage "doesn't look bad." Most hail damage is not visible from the ground. Granule loss, bruised shingles, cracked flashing, and compromised ridge caps require a professional to assess. What you can't see from your yard can still cost you thousands if it goes undocumented.
