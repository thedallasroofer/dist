North Texas sits in the heart of the country's most active hail corridor, commonly called "Hail Alley," with peak storm activity running from March through June each year. The Colony's position in southern Denton County. Directly adjacent to Frisco and along the same storm track that sweeps through Little Elm, Prosper, and into Collin County. Hail Damage Is Often Invisible from the Ground

Hail damage typically appears as granule loss on shingles, bruising or cracking of the shingle mat, and impacts on metal surfaces like gutters, downspouts, and AC units. None of which are visible from street level. By the time a water stain appears on a ceiling, the roof has often been compromised for weeks or longer. A professional inspection is the only reliable way to assess your Colony roof's actual condition after a storm event.

The Colony's Established Neighborhoods Have Roofs That Are Showing Their Age

The Colony is primarily a resale market. Most neighborhoods (Kings Crossing, Stewart Creek Estates, and established areas throughout the city) were built in the 1980s and 1990s, putting many roof systems 30-40+ years into a rated service life that runs 25-30 years under ideal conditions. North Texas hail seasons accelerate wear significantly. An aging roof that's taken multiple storm cycles isn't always visibly compromised. Storm Damage May Be Covered by Your Homeowner's Insurance

Standard homeowner's policies often cover hail and wind damage, but what's covered depends on your policy terms, deductible, roof condition, and your carrier's inspection findings.
